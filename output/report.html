Olist E-Commerce SQL Analytics

Seven business questions answered in PostgreSQL window functions and CTEs, on the public Olist Brazilian e-commerce dataset.

Analysis window: January 2017 through August 2018, delivered orders. Revenue means item price plus freight on delivered orders. Generated 2026-11-16 by scripts/run_analysis.py; every number on this page is query output.

Key findings

  • R$ 15.37 million of delivered revenue across 96,211 orders and 93,104 customers in 20 months (January 2017 through August 2018).
  • The best month was November 2017 with R$ 1.15 million, up 53.6 percent on the month before.
  • Repeat purchase is rare: 3.0 percent of customers ordered more than once in the window, and on average 0.48 percent of a cohort orders again in the month after the first purchase. No cohort cell after month 0 exceeds 0.72 percent.
  • The top two RFM segments by revenue (Recent high value and Lapsed high value) are 30.1 percent of customers and 55.6 percent of revenue.
  • Orders delivered 8 or more days late average a review score of 1.7 versus 4.32 for orders 8 or more days early; 69.8 percent of the late group leave 1-star reviews versus 6.5 percent.
  • Revenue is concentrated: the top 1 percent of sellers (30 sellers) earn 25.5 percent of revenue, the top 20 percent earn 81.5 percent.

1. Monthly revenue and order trend

How did monthly revenue, order count, and average order value develop, and how large are the swings month over month?

Revenue grew from R$ 127 thousand in January 2017 to a peak of R$ 1.15 million in November 2017, the Black Friday month, up 53.6 percent on October. Through 2018 monthly revenue settled around R$ 1.0 to 1.13 million with no further growth. Average order value stayed between R$ 146.25 and R$ 169.98 the whole time, so the growth in 2017 came from order volume.

2. Cohort retention

Of the customers who made their first purchase in a given month, what share ordered again in each later month?

Retention is very low in every cohort: on average 0.48 percent of a cohort orders again in the month after the first purchase, and the highest later-month cell in the whole table is 0.72 percent. Customers are identified by customer_unique_id; the per-order customer_id would show zero retention by construction. The numbers are reported as they are: this marketplace ran on one-time buyers during the window.

3. RFM segmentation

How do customers split by recency, frequency, and spend, and which segments carry the revenue?

Because 97.0 percent of customers bought exactly once, frequency quintiles would be arbitrary; frequency is scored by a direct rule (1, 2, or 3+ orders) and the segments are driven by recency and spend. The two high-value one-purchase segments hold 30.1 percent of customers and 55.6 percent of revenue. Both repeat segments together are 3.0 percent of customers and 5.6 percent of revenue, so there is no meaningful loyal core to defend yet.

4. Rolling and cumulative revenue

What does daily revenue look like smoothed over 30 days, and how does cumulative revenue grow?

The 30-day rolling sum peaks at R$ 1.31 million on 15 December 2017, right after the Black Friday spike. The date spine includes every calendar day, so days with no delivered orders count as zero in the window. The cumulative curve steepens through 2017 and grows at a near constant rate through 2018.

6. Delivery delay and review score

How do review scores differ between orders that arrived early, on time, or late?

Review scores fall steadily with lateness: orders 8 or more days early average 4.32, on-time orders 4.03, and orders 8 or more days late 1.7, with 69.8 percent of that group leaving 1-star reviews. 70,673 of 95,560 reviewed orders arrived 8 or more days early, so the platform pads its delivery estimates heavily. This is an association between lateness and scores; the data cannot establish causation.

7. Seller concentration

What share of revenue comes from the top 1, 5, and 20 percent of sellers?

The top 1 percent of sellers (30 of 2,945) earn 25.5 percent of revenue, and the top 20 percent earn 81.5 percent. That level of concentration means marketplace health depends on a few hundred sellers, and churn among the top tier would be felt directly in revenue.
